\section{Faithfulness and Sanity Protocols}
\label{sec:protocols}

An attribution map is only useful if it is \emph{faithful} to the model's actual mechanism and \emph{sensitive} to the factors that should govern it~\cite{eu2019trustworthy}. Both properties are normally tested on a single static prediction~\cite{samek2016evaluating,petsiuk2018rise,adebayo2018sanity}; since \fama's object of explanation is a multi-step trajectory and a signed scalar $\Delta M$ rather than a class score, we adapt both testing principles to that setting.

\subsection{Bidirectional Attribution Deletion Test (\biadt)}
\label{sec:biadt}

Classical deletion tests~\cite{samek2016evaluating,petsiuk2018rise} check one direction: does removing the top-ranked region degrade a prediction faster than removing a random one? $\PhiFama$ carries a \emph{sign} (\Cref{eq:fama_final}), so a faithful map must be validated in \emph{both} directions at once.

For each $x_k^S$, we segment it into $P$ SLIC superpixels~\cite{achanta2012slic} $\{s_p\}$, score each by its mean attribution $\bar\Phi(s_p)$, and sort ascending. Given a deletion ratio $\rho\in[0,1]$ and an order $\pi\in\{\texttt{desc},\texttt{asc},\texttt{rand}\}$ (most-positive-first, most-negative-first, or a random permutation averaged over repeats), we replace the first $\lfloor\rho P\rfloor$ superpixels with a blurred baseline to get $S_i^{(\rho,\pi)}$, recompute $\Delta M(\rho,\pi)$, and integrate the gap to the random control:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \mathrm{ABC}_{\downarrow} &:= \textstyle\int_0^1\!\big[\Delta M(\rho,\texttt{rand}){-}\Delta M(\rho,\texttt{desc})\big]d\rho,\\
    \mathrm{ABC}_{\uparrow} &:= \textstyle\int_0^1\!\big[\Delta M(\rho,\texttt{asc}){-}\Delta M(\rho,\texttt{rand})\big]d\rho.
\end{split}
\label{eq:abc_metric}
\end{equation}
A faithful map passes \biadt only if \emph{both} $\mathrm{ABC}_\downarrow$ (deleting the most helpful regions collapses $\Delta M$ faster than chance) and $\mathrm{ABC}_\uparrow$ (deleting the most harmful regions raises $\Delta M$ faster than chance) are positive and statistically significant -- a strictly stronger requirement than single-direction deletion tests, matched to the fact that $\PhiFama$ makes two claims (helpful \emph{and} harmful) rather than one.

\subsection{Sanity Checks}
\label{sec:sanity}

Passing \biadt shows $\PhiFama$ is causally linked to $\Delta M$ through $S_i$, but not that it reflects what $\theta_0$ actually \emph{learned} rather than architecture alone -- exactly the failure mode the parameter-randomization test of Adebayo~\etal~\cite{adebayo2018sanity} was designed to catch.

\paragraph{(a) Cascading randomization of $\theta_0$.} We reinitialize the backbone's last $\ell$ layers to fresh random weights, $\ell=1,\dots,L$, recompute $\Phi_{\mathrm{FAMA}}^{(\ell)}$ from scratch, and correlate it against the unperturbed map $\Phi_{\mathrm{FAMA}}$ (Spearman/Pearson, pixel-wise). A faithful map should decay toward the correlation of two independent random maps as $\ell$ grows; a map that stays highly correlated regardless of $\ell$ is a disguised edge detector, insensitive to $\theta_0$.

\paragraph{(b) Intervention on $S_i$.} In parallel, we perturb the \emph{content} of $S_i$ under three canonical forms of task heterogeneity~\cite{si2025meta}: \emph{(i) label permutation} (noisy task) -- shuffle class labels within $\mathcal{T}_i$; \emph{(ii) Gaussian blur} (hard task) -- degrade the discriminative signal directly; \emph{(iii) OOD task mixing} -- replace $S_i$ wholesale with a different task's support set while keeping $Q_i$ fixed. Each should push $\Delta M$ toward negative transfer; a faithful $\Phi_{\mathrm{FAMA}}'$ should diverge from $\Phi_{\mathrm{FAMA}}$ accordingly; a map whose correlation stays near 1 despite a semantically destructive edit has decoupled from $S_i$'s content.

Both checks are repeated over a large sample of meta-test tasks and reported as mean $\pm$ margin of error against the appropriate control, rather than on a handful of illustrative tasks (\Cref{sec:experiments}).
